## Audience

The client's owner or marketing lead at an industrial manufacturer. Smart,
busy, not an SEO. They have never heard of a "grounding query" and should not
need to. Write so the first page answers "are we showing up when AI answers
questions in our market, and is it getting better."

## Tone

Plain, direct, unhurried. No jargon without a plain-English gloss on first
use. Never use "GEO", "LLMO", "AI SEO" or similar coinages. Do not hype AI
search; do not dismiss it. State what is known, say what is uncertain, and
make the uncertainty specific rather than hedging every sentence.

## The honesty rule that governs this whole report

AI visibility data comes in two kinds and they must never be blended into one
number:

- **Measured** — something was recorded. Bing's citation data, Google
  Analytics sessions from AI assistants, and the answers we collected by
  asking the engines directly.
- **Inferred** — a guess from indirect evidence. Search Console query-syntax
  patterns are inference.

**Do not put inferred figures in this report at all.** They are an internal
research tool, too hedged to explain to a client, and mixing them with
measured numbers makes the measured ones less trustworthy. Report only what
was measured, and say where each number came from.

Where a number undercounts, say so in the same breath. Assistants that open
links without passing a referrer are recorded as direct traffic, so sessions
from AI are a floor, not a total.

## Sections, in this order

### 1. Where you stand

Three numbers, each one line, each naming its source and its period:

- Share of AI retrievals in your topics that cited you (Bing citation rate).
- How many of the buyer questions we track you are named in, out of the total
  tracked, and on which engines.
- Sessions that reached the site from an AI assistant (Google Analytics).

Then two or three sentences saying what those numbers mean together. If one
of the three has no data yet, say so plainly rather than omitting the line.

### 2. What buyers are asking, and whether you come up

The core of the report. Group the phrases AI uses to find companies like this
one into topics, and for each topic give the citation rate and the volume
behind it. Lead with the topics where the client is strongest, then the ones
where they are weakest and the gap is worth closing.

Explain in one sentence, once, that these are the search phrases the AI
itself generates when it goes looking for sources — not words a customer
typed. This distinction matters and the client will not know it.

Call out navigational brand phrases separately: being cited for the company's
own name is table stakes, and a rate below about 90% there is a problem worth
naming.

### 3. Who comes up instead

Competitors named in the answers we collected, how often, and for which
questions. If a competitor wins a topic the client should own, say which
topic and why it matters commercially. If there is no competitor data yet,
say that tracking needs to run longer rather than implying the field is
empty.

### 4. The pages AI actually relies on

Which of the client's pages get cited, and which pages AI assistants send
visitors to. Name the strongest two or three as assets worth protecting and
expanding. Where a page gets cited but receives no visits, say so — it means
the answer satisfied the reader without a click, which is normal now and not
a fault.

### 5. What changed

Compare with the previous report. If this is the first one, say so in a
sentence and set the expectation that trend is the point and it starts
accumulating now. Never present a first-period snapshot as a trend, and never
chart a period with no data as zero.

### 6. What we are doing next

Three to five concrete actions tied to specific findings above, each naming
the topic or page it addresses. No generic advice.

## Rules for figures

- Every number carries its source and period in the surrounding sentence.
- Percentages to one decimal at most; whole numbers for counts.
- A missing value renders as "—" and never as zero.
- No composite "AI visibility score". A blended index hides which input
  moved and cannot be acted on.
